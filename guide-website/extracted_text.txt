01 / BEFORE YOU START
04
YOU DO NOT NEED
TO KNOW EVERYTHING.
Your first year is about learning how to think, test and communicate - not collecting software and
architectural vocabulary.
01
LEARN ONE WORKFLOW
A strong sequence of decisions will save you more time than knowing ten programs badly.
02
LOOK AT REAL BUILDINGS
Plans, sections, details, materials and everyday use matter more than a beautiful hero render.
03
PRINT EARLY
A drawing that feels perfect on a laptop can completely fail at A1.
04
KEEP YOUR PROCESS
Save sketches, failed versions, models and diagrams. They show you how your thinking changes.
05
ASK SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
'How can I make it better?' is too broad. 'Does this entrance sequence work?' can actually move a project
forward.

05 / VISOID
08
VISUALISE AFTER
YOU HAVE A POINT OF VIEW.
A beautiful image should clarify architecture, not convince you to stop questioning it.
RITUAL OF THE CIRCLE / COMPLETE COURTYARD RENDER
MY ORDER
01  Decide what the view needs to explain.
02  Export a clear architectural base from Revit.
03  Use references intentionally: material, light, mood.
04  Explore visual directions in Visoid.
05  Choose and refine instead of generating endlessly.
06  Finish only what matters in Photoshop.
A GOOD RENDER CANNOT RESCUE A PROJECT YOU DO NOT UNDERSTAND.

09 / PROJECT START
15
DO NOT START
WITH PINTEREST.
Start by understanding the problem. Inspiration becomes useful only after you know what you are looking
for.
01
WHAT IS THE SITE DOING?
Light, access, slope, neighbours, noise, views, existing structure.
02
WHO IS THIS FOR?
Imagine real routines, not an abstract 'user'.
03
WHAT IS THE ONE IDEA?
If you cannot explain it in one sentence, it is probably not clear enough yet.
04
WHAT MUST THE PLAN SOLVE?
Circulation, privacy, daylight, dimensions, thresholds, storage.
05
WHAT MUST THE SECTION SOLVE?
Height, light, levels, structure, relation to landscape.
THEN LOOK FOR REFERENCES THAT ANSWER THOSE QUESTIONS.

11 / REFERENCES
17
DO NOT SAVE A BUILDING.
DISSECT IT.
01
What is the plan doing that mine is not?
02
Where is the main circulation sequence?
03
How does the section bring in light?
04
What is structural and what only looks structural?
05
Where do materials change - and why there?
06
How does the building meet the ground?
07
What happens at the door, window, corner and threshold?
08
What would still be good if the render was ugly?
A REFERENCE IS A QUESTION YOU CAN LEARN FROM.

14 / CRITS + SEMESTER RHYTHM
20
ARRIVE WITH
A QUESTION, NOT A PANIC.
FOR A CRIT
01
START WITH ONE SENTENCE
What is the project trying to do?
02
SHOW THE RIGHT DRAWING FIRST
The clearest drawing, not necessarily the prettiest.
03
ASK ONE SPECIFIC QUESTION
Make it possible to give you useful feedback.
04
WRITE FEEDBACK DOWN
You will not remember everything afterwards.
05
DO NOT CHANGE EVERYTHING Separate useful criticism from personal preference.
A SIMPLE WEEK
MON
define the question
TUE
references + fast options
WED
model / plan / section
THU
choose + develop
FRI
rough print / export
WEEKEND
edit + look again
A CRIT IS NOT A VERDICT ON WHETHER YOU ARE TALENTED.

16 / BEFORE SEMESTER STARTS
22
FIRST YEAR
STARTER CHECKLIST.
Install Revit and learn the basic interface.
Create one tiny practice model - not a full project.
Learn Photoshop selections, masks, layers and adjustment tools.
Learn pages, grids and paragraph styles in InDesign.
Bookmark 3-4 architecture reference sources you trust.
Study one building properly: plan + section + detail + photos.
Create a clean folder and file-naming system.
Set up automatic backups.
Remember: you are allowed to be a beginner.
